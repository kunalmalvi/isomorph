The bite limit was half the correct two-roll value, which made the old check too conservative. The new `2R(1 - 1/sqrt(1+mu^2))` matches the contact-length geometry `L = sqrt(R*dh)` already used in `roll_pass`. The formula is duplicated in the constraint engine, kept in sync by a test rather than shared code.

Parameter provenance is explicit and consistently labelled unverified. Nothing claims a checked source. Confidence, range basis and the "not certified" status appear in the data file, the JSON output, the report and the docs. The weakest values (silver K, roll radius, mill modulus, force limit) are marked low confidence or what-if.

The sensitivity module is domain-independent and deterministic. The demo's pass fractions depend entirely on analyst-judgment ranges and a triangular shape, which the report says. The report's explanatory sentences are partly static, and one reassurance line is thresholded at 50%.

## Bite limit correction shared between runner and constraint engine

For two rolls the draft is `dh = 2R(1 - cos(alpha))` with `tan(alpha) <= mu`, so the old `R(1 - cos)` was half the limit. At R = 100 mm and a 0.4 mm draft, the bite needs mu of about 0.063, which matches the note in `roller_problem.json`. The fix is applied in `max_bite_draft` and in a copy inside `constraint_engine/service.py` `_bite`. The duplication is tied together by `test_bite_helper_agrees_with_validator`, which uses cases on both sides of the limit, so reverting either copy fails a test. `test_reasoning.py` moves its violation cases from 0.5 to 0.8 reduction because the old values now satisfy the corrected limit (limit about 1.42 mm vs 1.0 mm draft at mu 0.12). Defaults of `friction=0.12` remain in `runners.py` (lines 263, 280, 293) for params that omit friction, which differs from the demo nominal 0.08 (possible inconsistency for callers that rely on defaults).

## Static sentences in the regenerated report

Most report sentences are conditional (`holds`/`does NOT hold`, `raise`/`do not raise`, nominal agreement). Three are not safe. The paragraph after the force table says low friction is the usual cause of a low pass fraction. A recomputation for copper gives bite-only pass fractions of 70%, 42.5% and 21.5% at 20, 30 and 40% reduction against force-only fractions of 100%, 99.5% and 45%. Bite dominates at these points, but roll radius is also sampled and affects bite, and silver rows at 30 to 40% are force-limited. The sentence should be derived from the two fractions already computed in `uncertainty_analysis`. The line "nominal verdict agrees with the majority" is true at 70% pass but gives the reader comfort the 30% failures do not support. The gauge conclusion fires at 95% of 60 samples, so a ten-sample shortfall would still print "holds".

## Sampling and percentile math

Percentiles use linear interpolation between order statistics. `rng.triangular(lo, hi, mode)` has the correct argument order and mode at nominal, and `sorted(ranges)` fixes draw order so the output is deterministic for a seed. The gauge comparison reuses the same samples and run seeds for both modes, so the paired "beats open loop" fraction is meaningful. `one_at_a_time` overrides every ranged parameter with its range nominal, so a base value that differs from the range nominal is silently replaced. K and n are sampled independently (issue 4). The roll-force model here is a plane-strain estimate, and K, n correlation affects the force spread most for silver, where K swing alone is 32.7 kN.

## Provenance claims

Copper values are attributed to Dieter with medium confidence and an analyst-judgment +/-15% range, and silver is explicitly derived with the arithmetic in `notes`. Friction is `kind: handbook` with confidence low and a source string that says "not checked". The `verification-status.md` table calls friction "low to medium" while the record says low. The silver K upper bound of 450 MPa is wider than the derived 270 to 366 MPa, which the notes say is for temper and purity. That choice is the analyst's, and it moves silver's 30% pass fraction to 18%.

Not tested: exact percentile values, triangular mode weighting, `n=0` (raises IndexError, unreachable from the demo), and both branches of the conditional report sentences.
